Prepared 21 September 2026, in response to Reviewer 3, weakness 3 ("legislative claims").
Every item below was checked at the primary source.

Verbatim §13 inserted into article 10 of Law 9.656/1998: *"Em caso de tratamento ou procedimento

## 2. Replacement text for §29

Current text, for comparison, ends: *"…the upward trajectory has persisted despite recent
regulatory interventions, including binding precedents from Brazil's Supreme Court and new
legislation in 2024.[7–9]"*

Proposed replacement for the whole paragraph:

> Between 2020 and 2024, new claims against health-plan operators more than doubled, from
> approximately 140 000 to 299 000 per year (an increase of 113%, closely matching the 112% growth
> reported independently by the IESS [5]), with the rate rising from 2.98 to 5.73 per 1 000
> beneficiaries. Part of the early increase reflects a pandemic-related backlog in 2020–2021, but
> the trend has continued: claims reached 341 763 in 2025. This persistence is notable because the
> observation window contains the two interventions that bear directly on supplementary-health
> coverage. Law 14.454 of 21 September 2022 amended Law 9.656/1998 to make the regulatory benefit
> package exemplary rather than exhaustive, requiring operators to authorise a prescribed treatment
> outside the package where its efficacy is supported by scientific evidence, or where the national
> health-technology assessment commission or a health-technology assessment body of international
> standing has recommended it.[7] In September 2025, ruling on Direct Action of Unconstitutionality
> 7265, the Supreme Federal Court upheld that provision while establishing cumulative criteria for
> coverage outside the package.[8] Neither intervention reversed the upward trajectory over the
> observation window.

Three things this fixes at once: it removes the misattributed legislation, it names the marker that
actually sits inside the series, and it stops describing SUS precedents as interventions in the
private market.

## 3. Reference-list changes

Remove the old ref 7 (Súmulas Vinculantes 60 and 61) and the old ref 9 (Law 14.874/2024). Insert
Law 14.454/2022 in position 7. The new entries, in the manuscript's existing style:

```
7. Brasil. Lei nº 14.454, de 21 de setembro de 2022. Diário Oficial da União.
   September 22, 2022.
8. Brasil. Supremo Tribunal Federal. Ação Direta de Inconstitucionalidade nº 7265.
   Relator Min. Luís Roberto Barroso. Julgamento concluído em September 18, 2025.
```

The list loses one entry, so everything from the old ref 10 onward shifts down by one: old 10–28
become 9–27. In-text citations to renumber: [5, 10] in §31, [11] in §31, [12–14] in §33, [19] in
§47, [20, 21, 22] in §53, and the block from [17] to [28] flagged separately in the minors. Do this
renumbering **once, at the end**, after all other text edits are final, and check it against the
in-text markers rather than against the list.

If the Súmulas Vinculantes are to be kept for context, they do not belong in §29. They would go in
§27, alongside the duplicative-coverage sentence, described accurately as precedents governing the
public system, with the corrected dates (DJE 16 September 2024 and 3 October 2024). My
recommendation is to drop them: the paragraph reads more cleanly without them, and the reviewer's
objection is precisely that public-system precedents were being presented as private-market
interventions.

## 4. One decision for the author

The submitted manuscript is written in American English ("Generalized", "prioritizing"). BMC
journals use British English. Switching the whole manuscript at revision stage is a large,
error-prone edit and BMC does not enforce it, so the replacement text above follows the
manuscript's existing American spelling for internal consistency. If you would rather convert,
that is a separate pass over the full text, not something to do paragraph by paragraph.
